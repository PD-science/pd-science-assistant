## Primary Article Discussed

- Mahul-Mellier et al. 2026, Science Advances.
- Title: Modeling Parkinson’s pathology in human iPSC dopaminergic neurons uncovers key mechanisms of Lewy body formation and heterogeneity
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aed8851

## Central Message

Lewy pathology should be understood as a dynamic continuum of inclusion states rather than a single static pathological entity. Inclusion maturation, alpha-synuclein assembly, PTM diversification, ultrastructural remodeling, and selective organelle recruitment jointly drive Lewy pathology heterogeneity.

## Key Concepts

- Lewy pathology includes somatic Lewy bodies and Lewy neurites.
- Alpha-synuclein is a major constituent of Lewy pathology and SNCA mutations/multiplications establish its central role in PD.
- Lewy pathology is heterogeneous in morphology, ultrastructure, molecular composition, anatomical distribution, and within-subtype variation.
- Inclusion state refers to a distinct biological configuration defined by molecular composition, structural organization, and interactions with the intracellular environment.

## Interpretation of Mahul-Mellier et al.

- Human iPSC-derived dopaminergic neuron culture plus alpha-synuclein PFF seeding allows pathology to develop from endogenous alpha-synuclein without overexpression.
- The model captures temporal evolution of human-like Lewy pathology.
- Pathology initially develops in neuronal processes, then extends to soma.
- Multiple inclusion morphologies coexist:
  - early filamentous assemblies.
  - later dense-like inclusions.
  - later ring-like inclusions.
  - increasingly complex membrane- and organelle-rich inclusions.
- PTM patterns include phosphorylation at multiple residues, ubiquitination, nitration, and C-terminal truncation.
- Organelle interactions are dynamic and compartment-specific.
- Lysosomal involvement is more prominent in mature inclusions.
- Organelle sequestration is selective rather than a uniform by-product of alpha-synuclein aggregation.

## Fibril versus Organelle Debate

The commentary frames Mahul-Mellier et al. as reconciling two views:

- Lewy bodies contain alpha-synuclein fibrils with central structural relevance.
- Lewy bodies also contain lipids, membrane fragments, and dysmorphic organelles.

The proposed synthesis is that fibrils and organelles contribute in different proportions and through partly distinct routes to inclusion formation.

## Therapeutic Implications

- Lewy pathology may not be a uniform therapeutic target.
- Passive anti-alpha-synuclein immunotherapies mainly target extracellular alpha-synuclein propagation.
- The intracellular processes shaping pathology after seeded aggregation may require different intervention strategies.
- Key therapeutic questions:
  - Which alpha-synuclein species and stages are toxic?
  - Which inclusion states are reversible?
  - Which inclusion states represent adaptive sequestration?
  - Which states directly disrupt neuronal homeostasis?
  - Should therapy prevent alpha-synuclein assembly, redirect inclusion maturation, or preserve compromised cellular pathways?

## Limitations

- The discussed model is an experimentally seeded neuronal system.
- It does not reproduce the multicellular and anatomical environment of the human brain.
- The relation between inclusion states, clinical manifestations, disease progression, and neurodegeneration remains unresolved.
