# Farrer 2026 unified evolutionary explanation of Parkinson's disease knowledge note

## Citation

- Title: A unified evolutionary explanation of Parkinson’s disease
- Author: Matthew J Farrer
- Journal: The Lancet Neurology
- Year: 2026
- Volume/pages: 25:802-803
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/S1474-4422(26)00296-6
- Article type: Insight / In Context / conceptual commentary

## Central Thesis

The article proposes that many Mendelian and risk-associated Parkinson's disease genes converge on an evolutionary mechanism involving pathogen defense, innate immunity, mitochondrial homeostasis, lysosomal homeostasis, and cellular senescence. The proposed overarching concept is antagonistic pleiotropy: alleles that improve survival early in life, potentially by enhancing infection resilience, can increase Parkinson's disease risk later in life.

## Key Genetic Points

- Population-specific variants discussed:
  - LRRK2 Arg1441Gly in Basques.
  - LRRK2 Gly2019Ser in Berbers and Ashkenazi Jews.
  - LRRK2 Gly2385Arg throughout Asian populations.
  - RAB32 Ser71Arg in Middle Eastern populations.
- Tunisian family data cited by the author:
  - about 40% of probands harbor LRRK2 Gly2019Ser.
  - 16% have PINK1 loss-of-function.
  - some have VPS35 Asp620Asn, RAB32 Ser71Arg, rarer causes including SNCA duplication.
- Mutational hotspots such as LRRK2 Arg1441Gly/Cys/His and VPS35 Asp620Asn are interpreted as suggestive of possible evolutionary advantage.

## Proposed Mechanism

- LRRK2, VPS35, and RAB32 heterozygous mutations can cause clinically idiopathic-like Parkinson's disease and lower the threshold for LRRK2 kinase activation.
- LRRK2 kinase maintains lysosomal membrane integrity and regulates lysosomal biogenesis through TFEB/TFE3-related transcription factors.
- VPS35, a retromer component, contributes to mitochondrial membrane repair and delivery of hydrolases including GBA1 to lysosomes.
- Retromer and LRRK2 influence TREM2 recycling in microglia and dopamine transporter recycling in dopaminergic neurons.
- In neurons, LRRK2 kinase-mediated RAB phosphorylation can impair synaptic vesicle cycling, axonal transport, and autophagy.
- RAB32 delivers mitochondrial itaconate to autophagosomes to restrict pathogen growth and can recruit/activate LRRK2 on lysosomal membranes in myeloid cells.
- PINK1 suppresses mitochondrial DNA release and cGAS/STING immune signaling; PINK1/Parkin mitophagy requires functional lysosomes.
- GBA1 and SNCA are also placed in an immune-regulatory framework.

## Research Framework

- Mendelian Parkinson's disease genes are framed as regulators of mitochondrial and lysosomal homeostasis after immune activation.
- Microglial activation is proposed to precede dopaminergic loss.
- Innate immunity is proposed as etiological, whereas sustained activation becomes pathological.
- Alpha-synuclein oligomerization is framed as secondary to microglial activation but still contributory because of alpha-synuclein's roles in innate and adaptive immunity.
- Penetrance in LRRK2 heterozygotes may depend on exposure to infectious disease, metabolic stress, or environmental toxicants.

## Proposed Biomarkers

- Reactive oxygen species production by neutrophils.
- Autophagy of bacteria by macrophages.
- Clearance of protein aggregates by microglia.

## Interpretive Links

The framework could help explain:

- population-specific penetrance in LRRK2 heterozygotes.
- disease-modifying effects of some anti-inflammatory drugs.
- body-first Parkinson's disease.
- inverse epidemiological associations with appendectomy or truncal vagotomy.
- possible roles of environmental pollutants.
- how GWAS loci may fit into immune and lysosomal biology.

## Limitations

- This is a conceptual commentary, not a direct experimental validation.
- The infection-resilience advantage remains a hypothesis requiring more evidence.
- The causal chain from immune activation to alpha-synuclein pathology to dopaminergic neuron loss needs cell-type-specific and longitudinal validation.
- Proposed biomarkers are mechanistically plausible but not established clinical biomarkers.
